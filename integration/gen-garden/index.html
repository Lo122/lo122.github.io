About Resume Integration Design

For our Zen garden, each interaction round starts with the human freely placing rocks in the sand pit. The AI then captures the updated arrangement through the camera and generates a new raking pattern in response. This pattern is sent to the robot, which uses a rake to draw it into the sand. After that, the human can place more rocks on top of the result, triggering the next design iteration.

Building on this interaction loop, our main challenges are: 1. how to make the communication between human and robot/AI fluent; 2. how to ensure the robot has accurately executed the assigned task.

For the first challenge, we employed AR headset to visualize and send signals for the human.

For the second, we implemented an AI self-evaluation step resulting in necessary corrective actions.

A key part of our work was building a reliable information exchange pipeline between the reality, python program, and grasshopper.

In total we have 4 types of communication, and for each communication link in the workflow, we define a specific method, device and variable name.

In our project, the agent's settings consist of a camera, UR robot, and AI.

Our agent completes a full feedback loop. It observes the scene with a camera, designs with AI, executes through the robot, and then self-evaluates using vision again.

The agent is capable of 3 types of generation, based on Gemini's "gemini-2.5-flash-image", it can:

1. generate image out of the given prompt and a input image;

2. detect the raking patterns and isolate them as red curves, then generating another image only showing the red curves which facilitates the OpenCV detection in the following steps.

3. evaluate the robot's completion quality by taking one captured image, one generated image, a metrics calculated by skimage, and a given prompt.
